# Q2.5 -- predictions written BEFORE running any experiment of "Testing the Baseline"

(State of knowledge when written: Table 1, Figure 1 and Table 2 / Figure 2 exist. Nothing from Tables 3-4 or
Figures 3-5 has been computed. **Disclosure:** `src/train.py` prints, for monitoring, the *validation-split*
E^(2) at 2x and 3x after every epoch; I saw these numbers while model A trained (about 0.043 and 0.051, against
about 0.022 for E^(5) at 1x), so the speed part of this prediction is *not* fully blind. The mirror/rotation part is.)

Facts I rely on: model A's one-step reach is 8 original pixels (2 ConvLSTM layers x 3x3 kernels x 4-pixel cells,
Q2.3); the typical digit speed is 3.3 px/frame (Figure 1b, 95th percentile 4.3), so at 2x it is about 6.6 px per seen
frame (fastest digits about 8.6) and at 3x about 10 px (fastest about 13); Figure 1a is flat within sampling noise, so
mirrored and rotated *trajectories* are as likely as the original ones.

**Mirror test.** Model A will mostly pass: the motion statistics are unchanged (only the digits' appearance changes), so
I expect E_M to be somewhat larger than E^(5) (about 1.1-1.5x) and C_M clearly non-zero but small (roughly 10-30 % of
E^(5)); both far better than Copy-last. It will not be exactly consistent.

**Rotation test.** Model A will do worse than on the mirror test (E_R about 1.5-3x E^(5), C_R larger than C_M): rotation
changes the appearance more (stroke orientation changes; mirroring keeps vertical strokes vertical), and a ConvLSTM that
partly memorised digit shapes to keep them sharp will blur or distort rotated digits. The motion rule itself should
carry over, so I still expect it to stay better than Copy-last.

**Speed.** It will be fine at 1x, degrade noticeably at 2x (never seen in training and the fastest digits already
reach the 8 px limit; still better than Copy-last), and break down at 3x, where nearly every digit moves further than
the 8 px reach between seen frames: E^(2) no better than Copy-last, and visible failures (a digit that stalls near its
last seen position, ghosting/blur, or one that vanishes). So the breakdown speed should lie between 2x and 3x, i.e.
at an apparent displacement of about 8 px per seen frame.
